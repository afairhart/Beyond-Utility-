01

Company Overview

Biota, Inc. is a Delaware C-corp based in Longmont, CO. Rose Nash founded it in 2024 out of the Antler Boulder residency. The long-term vision is a platform, the "Abbott or BD of environmental diagnostics," with a set of products per contaminant from field screening up to regulated-grade testing. Microplastics is the likely second platform (dev in 2027, launch in 2028). Two PFAS products are commercializing now.

Product · screening lane

RapidTest™

A protein-binding fluorescence displacement assay that runs on a ~$3K off-the-shelf fluorimeter. Total-PFAS readout in under 15 minutes at about $10 per analysis (~$30 per sample in triplicate). Kits of 50 tests at $500, over 80% gross margin. It screens; it does not replace regulated LC-MS/MS.

Product · lab workflow

MagnaPrep™

Magnetic nanoparticles coated with proprietary WAX-like "head" binders and fluorous "tail" binders (about 200 formulations screened) that concentrate PFAS roughly 1000x in place of SPE. About 90 samples/hr vs 8 per shift. Short-chain recovery goes from about 2% to about 98%. $450 per bottle (~$15/sample), over 80% margin. Drops into EPA Method 1633 workflows.

The flywheel: RapidTest drives a 10 to 50x jump in sampling volume, some of those samples go to the lab, MagnaPrep speeds the confirmatory LC-MS/MS step, and Biota gets written into the project. The eventual MagnaPrep into RapidTest integration (preconcentration feeding the field assay for low-ppt) is the key piece of the platform, and it is not built yet.

02

Why Now and Market

PFAS testing is a modest market today, about $0.5B across the US and Europe, and almost all of it is grab-sample-and-ship to a lab against a regulated method (1633) at $300 to $500 per test with a 2 to 6 week turnaround (rush fees run 50 to 300%). The opportunity is the unserved need for fast, cheap, in-field screening among the operators for whom turnaround drives project cycle time: remediation and engineering firms, PFAS destruction and treatment operators, landfill and industrial dischargers.

The regulatory path is better than a pure compliance-lane read

For drinking water, Biota's tech would have to be written into an EPA-approved (prescriptive) method, which is roughly a 5-year path. But for non-drinking-water matrices (wastewater, leachate, biosolids, soil), Method 1633 is performance-based. Labs can adopt new sample prep like MagnaPrep in weeks through their own validation, with no central EPA certification. That is the beachhead, and it skips the multi-year clearance that kills most environmental-tech timelines.

  • Demand support is broad, if uneven. EPA's "PFAS OUT" initiative and a joint EPA/HHS $144M emerging-contaminants program (Apr 2026), PFAS on the Contaminant Candidate List, and PFAS legislation active in about 39 states create state-level momentum that does not depend on federal action. Litigation against the likes of 3M is a non-regulatory driver of testing demand.
  • Timing risk is still here. Federal drinking-water treatment deadlines (2029) could slip, and the binding compliance market is served by LC-MS/MS, not by Biota. The honest "why now" is part regulatory, part economic, and the beachhead is the economic, screening side.

Sizing: Biota's bottom-up SAM is about $880M (RapidTest) plus about $60M US (MagnaPrep). As with the Fluorityx pitch I looked at, the big RapidTest layers (operational and wastewater monitoring) lean on routine ppt-grade monitoring that needs both regulation and the unbuilt ppt capability. The serviceable-today slice (remediation plus tech-dev screening, where ppb works) is the real near-term market. I'm underwriting to that slice and treating the rest as upside.

03

Technology and Validation

RapidTest uses a human transport protein (transthyretin family) loaded with a quenched fluorescent reporter. PFAS competitively displace the reporter and fluorescence drops. The mechanism is clean and the class reactivity is broad, but it is a one-to-one interaction with no signal amplification. MagnaPrep's dual head-and-tail ligand chemistry is the bigger differentiator and the stronger near-term wedge.

The sensitivity question is a risk I can manage, not a dealbreaker

Standalone RapidTest sits at about 5 ppb, against the 4 ppt drinking-water MCL. The key point from Burnt Island's customer work is that the beachhead buyers (remediation, engineering, treatment) do not need ppt. A lot of their work is in the ppb range, and what they want is a fast result that holds up against 1633. Field-deployable low-ppt through MagnaPrep integration is upside I am not paying for in the base case. I track it; I do not depend on it.

  • Real-matrix performance is the live technical risk. Until about 8 Apr 2026, all testing was on synthetic and contrived waters. The first real sample (Aclarity leachate) performed well, and 12 spiked samples across varied pH, ionic strength, and TOC collapsed to under 5% RSD. The dataset is still thin, and surfactant interference above the CMC is a known boundary condition. The mitigations (dilution, a correction algorithm, MagnaPrep cleanup) are sensible but not yet proven across many matrices.
  • Validation is in-house, run on an entry-level Waters Xevo TQ-S micro. Independent, third-party, dirty-matrix benchmarking against 1633 is still open. The assay misses GenX and short-chain PFBA (panel is C4 and up), and TFA is out of scope.

Intellectual Property and Defensibility

The IP posture got better during Burnt Island's diligence, which itself tells me Rose takes input.

  • Two omnibus provisionals are in preparation with experienced counsel, one for MagnaPrep and one for RapidTest. They cover composition of matter (ligand chemistry, assay components), process (extraction and detection workflows), and system-level claims (the MagnaPrep plus RapidTest integration), and they extend MagnaPrep chemistry to PFAS removal and treatment, which widens the strategic value. This is a move off trade-secret-only, prompted by the lead.
  • Existing filing: provisional US 63/880,119 (filed 11 Sep 2025) on photoresponsive elution for MagnaPrep. It abandons by about Sep 2026 unless converted, and the round funds the conversion and PCT.
  • Caveat: these are still provisional and unexamined, the core RapidTest chemistry was trade-secret until now, and first-mover, flywheel, and brand matter as much as the patents. I want to confirm the two filings are actually submitted (targeted within weeks of the BIV memo) before close.

Business Model and Unit Economics

Recurring consumables, low COGS, over 80% gross margin, priced at or below the incumbent so adoption needs no budget increase. This is the cleanest part of the company, and it fits the historically high margins of analytical-instrument businesses (Waters, Beckman, Hach), which is also why the strategic exits pay well.

08

Competition

The lead competitor is faltering

FREDsense

Raised a $7M Series A (Sep 2025, HG Ventures and Emerald) but is struggling. President Eric Stoermer left in Mar 2026. Cornelsen UK called the product hard to use and stopped using their unit. $16K instrument, $100 per test, about 3 hours of prep. Biota is cheaper, faster, and reportedly better in dirty matrices, which opens a window to be first with something that works.

The broader field

Incumbents and emerging sensors

SPE incumbents (Waters, Agilent, Thermo, Phenomenex) for MagnaPrep, with no magnetic-nanoparticle analytical competitor today. Salvus, Fluorityx (my parallel diligence), and academic electro and MIP sensors for RapidTest. Battelle and Thermo work instrumentation, not field tests. SimpleLab and CycloPure are mail-in, so possible channels rather than rivals.

The position is first mover with a working, low-cost field product plus a defensible sample-prep wedge. The moat is flywheel, brand, and IP rather than one patent, which fits the stage.

Pre-Mortem

It is 2029 and this is a write-down. The likeliest stories, and what I'd lean on against each:
  • Real-matrix performance does not generalize. The assay works in synthetic and early leachate tests but stumbles across messy real matrices (surfactant interference). Against it: dilution, the correction algorithm, and MagnaPrep cleanup. I'll watch the validation dataset grow over the next two quarters as a gating signal.
  • Market creation is slow. Buyers used to 1633-certified results are slow to trust owning the testing, and labs resist switching off SPE. Against it: MagnaPrep's drop-in 1633 compatibility and the government-contract channel. I underwrite to the near-term slice, not the big SAM.
  • Solo-founder execution. A first-time founder under cash pressure mis-hires or stalls through the first crises. Against it: the board seat (BIV) and my own engagement. This is the core risk of the bet.
  • Pricing and volume. $500 kits need huge unit volume to scale, and viral adoption underdelivers. Against it: the demonstrated pricing room and the higher-priced ppt product to come.
  • The ppt bridge never closes and the bigger operational and utility market never opens. I can live with that, because I am not paying for it in the base case.
