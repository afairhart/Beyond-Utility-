Instructions (delete in final). This template is used at Stage 5 of the BUV investment pipeline. Target length: 4–5 pages plus appendix. Each traditional section closes with a reference to the relevant BUV Six criteria scored on a four-tier scale (Strong Pass / Pass / Concern / Fail). The Three Ways This Goes to Zero pre-mortem and BUV Six Scorecard sections are mandatory. This memo triggers mandatory advisor review before capital is committed.
Section 01

Executive Summary & Recommendation

One paragraph. What does the company do, in plain language? What is the investment thesis in one sentence? What is the recommendation and the key reasons for it? If recommending Pass or Defer, state that explicitly. This is the only section an advisor may read in isolation, so it must stand alone.

Section 02

Company Overview

Two to three paragraphs. Founding date, location, founding team composition at a high level, stage of development, current capitalization, what the company has built to date. Prior financings if any — amount, investors, structure. This section is factual, not evaluative.
Section 03

Market & Why Now

Two to three paragraphs. Describe the specific market this company addresses, the buyer the company sells to first, and the forcing function creating urgency now. Include the regulatory, economic, or operational trigger that compels adoption within a definable window. Size the beachhead bottom-up — not TAM from a market research report, but a grounded estimate of the specific first-customer segment. Name the demand pull explicitly; if the company is selling into push-based dynamics, that is itself the key risk and must be called out.
BUV Six addressed: 02 Mandated or Forced Demand · 03 Narrow Beachhead

Section 04

Team & Founder

Two to three paragraphs. Founders — names, backgrounds, roles, prior operating or research experience, demonstrated domain credibility. How did they come to this problem? What is their unfair insight? Include reference call notes: who did BUV speak to about each founder, what did they say, what stood out. Note specifically whether references spontaneously described founders as "the real deal" or equivalent, versus polite but uncommitted language. Flag any gaps in the team and how the company plans to close them.
BUV Six addressed: 01 Sector-Earned Credibility · 05 The Buyer Leans In
Section 05

Product & Technology

Three to four paragraphs. What is the product, technically? What problem does it solve and how? What is the state of development — concept, prototype, pilot, commercial? What is the intellectual property position — patents granted or pending, claims scope, freedom-to-operate analysis, trade secret posture? Who did BUV speak to on technical diligence, and what did they say? For any novel scientific or engineering claim, note whether it has been independently validated, and if not, what validation would be required.

Be specific about technology readiness level (TRL). For anything that depends on a scientific claim BUV cannot directly validate, name the advisor who reviewed it.
BUV Six addressed: 01 Sector-Earned Credibility · 04 Systems Thinking
BUV IC Memo 03
Section 06

Business Model & Unit Economics

Two to three paragraphs. How does the company make money — product sale, recurring service, subscription, water-as-a-service, data licensing, other. What are the unit economics at the beachhead customer: price per unit or contract, gross margin, cost of customer acquisition, time from first contact to revenue. Does the pricing reflect realistic water-sector procurement cadence, or does it assume software-style velocity that will not materialize? What is the path from pilot to commercial, and is the pilot structured to convert? Who installs, services, and renews? Use of funds from this round — what milestone does the capital buy, and is that milestone fundable by Series A investors in BUV's network?
BUV Six addressed: 04 Systems Thinking · 03 Narrow Beachhead
Section 07

Competition & Defensibility

Two to three paragraphs. Who are the direct competitors? What are the named incumbents — Xylem, Veolia, SUEZ, Ecolab, etc. — and how would they respond to this company's traction? What is this company's structural defensibility at the beachhead: intellectual property with enforceable claims, data or network effects that compound with use, certification or regulatory head start, channel or integrator relationships, switching costs. Be specific about what prevents a well-resourced incumbent from replicating the offering within 18 months. If nothing does, that is the key risk.
BUV Six addressed: 03 Narrow Beachhead, Hard for Incumbents to Serve
BUV IC Memo 04
Section 08

Traction & Diligence Findings

Two to three paragraphs. What has the company actually sold or deployed? Pilot customers, letters of intent, signed contracts, revenue — with amounts and customer names where permissible. Consolidated findings from buyer reference calls: what specific buyers said, how they described the founder and the product, whether they intend to deploy commercially after pilot, whether they would champion the technology internally. Include direct quotes from reference calls where the phrasing is distinctive. Flag any dissonance between what the founder claims and what buyers say.
BUV Six addressed: 05 The Buyer Leans In · 02 Mandated or Forced Demand
Section 09

Deal Terms & Syndicate

One to two paragraphs. Round size, pre-money valuation, vehicle (SAFE with cap/discount, priced round, convertible note), post-money, proposed BUV check, resulting BUV ownership, pro-rata rights secured, information rights, board posture (seat, observer, or neither), other key terms. Co-investors in the round — who is leading, who is following, whether any strategic investor is in. Brief assessment of whether the valuation is defensible relative to comparable water-sector seed rounds and the company's stage of development.

Section 10

Three Ways This Goes to Zero
Pre-Mortem

Mandatory section. In three short paragraphs, describe the three most plausible scenarios in which this investment returns zero to BUV. Do not include generic venture risks. Be specific to this deal.

01 · The First Scenario

What would cause this? What diligence reduces the probability? What does the company need to achieve before this concern is retired?

02 · The Second Scenario

What is the evidence one way or the other? What is the timeline to know?

03 · The Third Scenario

Which actor or dynamic? What would trigger their response or its emergence? What defensibility does BUV believe holds up against this scenario?

This section exists to force honest underwriting and to create a baseline against which BUV can evaluate the investment in year three. If the scenarios ever actually play out, this section is what the retrospective begins with.

Overall Pattern

Two to three sentences on the scoring pattern. If there are concerns or fails, how material are they, and what would change the score? BUV does not require six Strong Passes to invest — a deal with one Concern and a credible path to resolution can still be a compelling investment. The scorecard's purpose is disciplined honesty, not a hurdle.
Section 12

Exit Scenarios

One to two paragraphs. Named strategic acquirers with rationale for why each would care: what specific strategic gap does this company fill for them, what is their recent M&A pattern, what has been paid for comparable companies in the last five years. BUV requires at least three credible acquirers named at underwriting; if that cannot be done, the deal fails criterion #6 and should not be invested.

Appendices

Supporting Diligence

Appendix A — Reference Calls

List each reference call conducted during diligence, with date, reference name and role, their relationship to the company or founder, and a brief summary of what they said. For buyer reference calls, include direct quotes where they are distinctive. Backdoor references are noted separately from founder-provided references.

Appendix B — Technical Diligence Notes

Detailed technical diligence findings beyond what fits in Section 05. IP review summary, FTO analysis, pilot data review, comparative technology analysis as relevant.

Appendix C — Market Sizing

Bottom-up beachhead sizing methodology and assumptions. Beachhead segment definition, number of target customers, price per customer, realistic capture percentage, resulting beachhead revenue opportunity.

Appendix D — Competitive Map

Table or detailed list of direct and adjacent competitors with positioning, funding stage, and differentiation.

Appendix E — Deal Documents

List of deal documents reviewed: term sheet, prior financing documents, cap table, key customer contracts, IP assignments, etc.
